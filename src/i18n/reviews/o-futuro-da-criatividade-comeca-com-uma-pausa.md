# The future of creativity begins with a pause.

We spend a good part of the day producing. Creating layouts, searching for references, solving problems, and trying to find the next good idea.

But there's a question we rarely ask ourselves: are we dedicating enough time to thinking?

## Creating is not just producing.

Speed has become a constant requirement in the creative market. We need to deliver more, adapt more, and learn more.

But creativity doesn't depend exclusively on the quantity of things we produce.

It also depends on what we observe, study, and are able to connect.

## Repertoire is a construction.

A good idea can be born from a conversation, a book, an exhibition, or something seemingly unrelated to design.

The more references we have, the more possibilities we can see.

Therefore, building a repertoire is also part of an art director's job.

> Not every pause represents an interruption. Some are an essential part of the creative process.

## And what remains of all this?

Perhaps the challenge isn't finding ways to produce more and more.

Perhaps it's about learning to produce with more intention.

Creating requires technique, sensitivity, research, and time to transform information into something relevant.

And sometimes, it all starts with a pause.
